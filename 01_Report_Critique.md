# Critique: "Pan-European Biomethane Market Analysis & Comprehensive Trading Strategy (2026–2027)"
**Fact-check and integration notes · 16 August 2026**

---

## Verdict

Genuinely useful — roughly 70% solid, and it contains at least five things I'd missed that materially improve your plan. The country-by-country sweep, the counterparty directory and the margin allocation matrix are real work and worth keeping.

But it has **two errors that would cost you money in a meeting**, both in the two markets your KPIs care most about, and it was written with no knowledge of who 3Degrees actually is — so its entire execution plan (Section 7) assumes a merchant trading desk with a balance sheet you may not have.

Use it as a **market atlas**. Do not use it as a **strategy**.

---

## Part 1 — Errors, ranked by what they'd cost you

### 🔴 1. The Netherlands section describes a system being abolished

**The report says:** the Dutch market is "defined by the HBE system", "HBE-G (advanced) tickets remain highly lucrative", and builds two CRM pipeline rows and an entire arbitrage case study on selling into HBE-G.

**Reality:** the HBE system is being **scrapped**. The Netherlands is transitioning to an **Emissions Reduction Obligation** denominated in **EREs** (Emission Reduction Units), where 1 ERE = 1 kg CO₂-equivalent avoided versus fossil. The 2025 compliance year closes out under HBE; **deliveries from 1 January 2026 must comply with the new rules**, and the new registry goes live **May 2026**. Under the new system there are **no multipliers and no double counting**. RFNBOs lose their multiplier and instead generate RAREs, a separate ERE category.

**Why this matters to you:** the Netherlands is the ARA bunkering hub and one of your two named KPI markets by implication. Walking into a Dutch counterparty and pricing in HBE-G language in October 2026 would mark you immediately as someone working off stale material. Worse, the *unit of account changes* — from a volumetric ticket to a mass of CO₂e avoided — which changes how you price low-CI molecules. That's a structural shift in your favour if you understand it and a trap if you don't.

**Action:** rebuild the Dutch section from scratch. The ERE transition is a **week 5 priority**, promoted from background reading.

---

### 🔴 2. German double counting — the report is internally contradictory, and the truth is genuinely unresolved

**The report says**, in the same document: that manure biomethane "benefits from double-counting multipliers", that Trade 1 captures "the advanced multiplier premium" — *and* that traders "must navigate the impending removal of certain double-counting mechanisms."

It can't be both. And it builds its headline German arbitrage on the version that's going away.

**What's actually confirmed:** double counting of advanced biofuels toward the THG quota is eliminated **from the 2026 compliance year**, not applying to fuels placed on the market before 1 January 2026. The German Cabinet approved the draft on 10 December, setting a 59% GHG reduction trajectory by 2040, adding a binding SAF mandate and new fraud-prevention measures. Removing double counting was explicitly intended to stop over-compliance, which had been depressing quota prices.

**What is genuinely uncertain — and I was too confident about this last time:** there is reporting that **biomethane specifically may be exempt and may remain eligible for double counting**. That is not settled, and I overstated it in the first plan by saying flatly that double counting was abolished.

**Why this matters enormously:** whether biomethane keeps the multiplier is the single largest swing factor in German biomethane value. It roughly *doubles or halves* the tCO₂e generated per MWh, and therefore the THG revenue per MWh. Your KPI names a THG deal explicitly.

**Also worth separating — the report conflates two different things:**
- **Double counting** — a *policy multiplier* applied to Annex IX feedstocks. Being removed (biomethane status TBC).
- **Manure's negative carbon intensity** — not a multiplier at all, but the *physics of the GHG calculation*. Manure earns credit for avoided methane emissions, giving a strongly negative CI, which mechanically generates far more tCO₂e of reduction per MWh. This is unaffected by the double-counting decision.

Knowing that distinction cleanly is a genuine credibility marker. Most people muddle it — as this report does.

**Action:** this is **question one** for your manager and for any German counterparty. Establish the current legal position before you price anything.

### 🟠 3. The arbitrage case studies don't add up

Trade 1: buy Spanish manure biomethane at **TTF + €40**, sell into German THG at **TTF + €95**. That's a **€55/MWh spread**. The report then concludes the desk captures **"€3–€5/MWh net margin."**

Phase 2 repeats the incoherence: offer Spanish producers **TTF + €35**, sell into Germany at **TTF + €80** — a **€45 spread** — while the whole plan is built on a **€2.60/MWh** average margin.

You cannot have both. There aren't €50/MWh of costs sitting between those two prices.

**Why it matters:** at €45/MWh margin you'd hit €1.35m on **30,000 MWh** — and the 500,000 MWh volume target would be trivially easy, which it obviously isn't. The two numbers describe two completely different businesses:

- **€2–3/MWh** = you're an intermediary, matching and clearing, thin margin, high throughput. Consistent with the KPI structure.
- **€45–55/MWh** = you're taking origination risk, prepaying producers, warehousing certificates and carrying regulatory risk. Requires balance sheet.

**Which one you actually are is a question for 3Degrees, not for a report.** But note: the KPI ratio (€1.35m ÷ 500,000 MWh = €2.70/MWh) tells you the company is modelling the *thin* version. Don't quote the fat spread numbers to anyone.

### 🟠 4. FuelEU: the penalty framing is backwards, and it gives away your best argument

**The maths checks out.** €2,400 per tonne of VLSFO-equivalent ÷ 41,000 MJ/tonne = €0.0585/MJ × 3,600 MJ/MWh ≈ **€210/MWh**. Correct.

**But the report calls this "an absolute price ceiling for Bio-LNG" and proposes selling at a 15% discount to it. That's the wrong way round, and it leaves money on the table.**

€210/MWh is the penalty on **non-compliant energy**. But FuelEU sets a *percentage GHG-intensity reduction* target across the vessel's whole energy consumption. So a **small** quantity of very-low-CI bio-LNG neutralises a **much larger** compliance deficit. The value per MWh of bio-LNG you deliver is therefore the penalty avoided divided by the MWh you supplied — which can be **well above €210/MWh**, not capped by it.

**The report also missed the progressive penalty multiplier:** penalties escalate by 10%, 20%, 30% and upward for consecutive years of non-compliance. A repeat offender's willingness to pay is structurally higher than a first-timer's — that's a segmentation insight worth real margin.

**Action:** build the FuelEU penalty-avoidance calculator yourself in week 4. Do not price off the report's ceiling.

---

### 🟡 5. Maersk is the wrong first call

The report ranks **Maersk** first among downstream buyers with "massive Bio-LNG appetite."

Maersk's dual-fuel newbuild programme has been predominantly **methanol**-led. **CMA CGM** and **MSC** have the larger LNG dual-fuel fleets and are the more natural bio-LNG buyers. The report lists them second and third.

Reorder your maritime outreach accordingly. Opening a Maersk conversation with a bio-LNG pitch risks looking like you haven't read their fleet strategy.

---

### 🟡 6. The non-EU constraint is understated to the point of being misleading

The report treats the **UK, Norway and Switzerland** as live trading partners — the UK "continues to be a major off-taker for advanced EU biomethane" — and mentions "non-EU mass balancing interfaces" only vaguely.

It never states the hard rule: **biomethane injected into a non-EU gas grid cannot currently be recorded in the Union Database**, which limits its eligibility for EU compliance reporting under RED III. That's a deal-killer, not an administrative inconvenience. Certification of non-EU value chains under ISCC EU is permitted where supply chains are segregated or use recognised mass-balance systems — but the UDB recording gap is the binding constraint.

Any structure routing UK/CH/NO molecules into EU compliance needs this checked first, not last.

---

### 🟡 7. ETS2 is entirely absent

The report has a whole customer segment called "Industrial / Corporate ETS Compliance & Scope 1" but never distinguishes **ETS1** (current, installations, where biomass zero-rating requires RED compliance) from **ETS2** (buildings and road transport fuel suppliers) — and never mentions that **ETS2 was postponed by one year to 2028** by Council and Parliament in March 2026.

This is a conspicuous gap given **3Degrees has published its own piece on ETS2 and why biomethane matters for it**. If your new employer is positioning around ETS2 and your research report doesn't mention it, that's the gap to close before day one.

---

### ⚪ 8. Invented precision — useful directionally, never quotable

These read as confident but have no visible source:

- "**5–10% risk premium**" on ex-domain transactions
- "**25 days or more**" for ERGaR↔AIB transfers
- Carbon intensity of "**exactly −100 gCO₂e/MJ**" for manure (real values vary widely and are calculated per pathway)
- The specific premium bands: €80–100/MWh manure, €40–60/MWh generic waste, €10–20/MWh GOs

Treat all of these as hypotheses to verify against a live price subscription. Never as figures to quote to a counterparty.

**Also note:** the report's "5.2–6.0 bcm production" and the EBA's "8.2 bcm" are **not contradictory** — one is production, the other installed capacity, implying ~70% utilisation. But the report doesn't make the distinction, and quoting the wrong one makes you look sloppy.

---

### ⚪ 9. Tone

"Massive", "staggering", "draconian", "exorbitant", "aggressively", "effortlessly surpass the CY2027 target." Do not paste any of this into anything a manager or counterparty reads. Trading notes should read flat and precise; confidence comes from the numbers, not the adjectives.

## Part 2 — What it got right that I missed

Credit where it's due. Five of these are going straight into the plan.

### ✅ 1. The French CPB scheme — the biggest thing I missed

I had France down as "TIRUERT, moving to IRICC, delayed to 2027" and effectively parked it. That was wrong. There's a **second, separate French mechanism** that is live right now:

**Certificats de Production de Biogaz (CPB)** — created by the 2021 Climate and Resilience Law, **in force from 1 January 2026**. Natural gas suppliers must surrender certificates annually in proportion to volumes delivered to residential and tertiary customers.

Verified detail, sharper than the report's:
- Penalty of **€100 per CPB not surrendered** — which functions as a **hard price cap** at €100/MWh, because no supplier rationally pays more than the penalty
- First restitution period runs **1 Jan 2026 – 31 Dec 2028**
- First declaration to DGEC due **1 March 2027** — *four months into your job*
- In 2026 the obligation applies **only to suppliers selling more than 400 GWh/year**, with the exemption threshold falling each year to **zero by year five**
- **EEX operates the registry**

**Why this is a big deal for you:** it's a new, obligation-driven demand pool switching on at exactly the moment you start, with a defined price ceiling, a known first-compliance deadline inside your first six months, and an identifiable, finite list of obligated parties (French gas suppliers above 400 GWh). That is about as clean an origination target as this market offers. The report deserves full credit for surfacing it.

### ✅ 2. ERGaR vs AIB — the registry fragmentation

I mentioned ERGaR. I didn't cover that the market is split between **ERGaR** (which handles the bulk of historical biomethane pipeline transfers, especially Denmark→Germany and Denmark→UK) and the **AIB**, which dominated renewable *electricity* GOs and is now scaling its EECS standard to gases and pushing national registries to consolidate under it.

The commercial consequence is real: you may need accounts or partners on **both** hubs to avoid liquidity fragmentation, and cross-hub transfers involve manual intervention, settlement lag and administrative basis risk. (Treat the specific "25 days" as unverified.)

### ✅ 3. Ex-domain cancellations

A genuine operational mechanism I hadn't covered. Where registries aren't directly integrated or export is restricted — **France, Finland, Spain** — value moves by cancelling the certificate in the origin registry and transferring the cancellation statement to the destination registry for consumption. Ownership isn't guaranteed until the receiving registry accepts.

**Gasgrid Finland publishes formal ex-domain cancellation instructions** — a concrete, readable primary document. Good week-2 material.

### ✅ 4. VertiCer — the report is right and I was wrong

I wrote "Vertogas" as the Dutch registry. **CertiQ and Vertogas merged to form VertiCer on 1 January 2023**, and the separate registries were integrated into a single **myVertiCer** platform from **1 July 2025**. Correcting this in the plan.

### ✅ 5. The margin allocation matrix — the report's single best contribution

| Segment | Volume | Margin | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesale / utilities | 250,000 MWh | €1.00/MWh | €250,000 |
| Transport compliance | 150,000 MWh | €3.00/MWh | €450,000 |
| Maritime bio-LNG | 100,000 MWh | €6.00/MWh | €600,000 |
| **Total** | **500,000 MWh** | **€2.60/MWh** | **€1,300,000** |

The specific numbers are speculative. **The structure is exactly right** and matches the conclusion I reached independently: a thin-margin high-volume wholesale base to carry the MWh target, with concentrated margin in compliance and maritime. Adopt the framework, replace the numbers with verified ones as you get price discovery.

Note it totals €1.30m — just under the €1.35m KPI. Worth showing your manager as evidence the target is tight, not comfortable.

### ✅ 6. Tier 2/3 texture I'd deferred to week 5

- **Ireland** — Renewable Heat Obligation, forcing heat suppliers to blend, turning Ireland from producer into net importer *(timing needs verification — this has slipped before)*
- **Austria** — EGG green gas supplier quota rising to **7.7% by 2030**; OMV as the obvious obligated buyer
- **Sweden** — biomethane tax exemption annulled by EU courts then **re-approved by the Commission**, reviving imports from Denmark
- **Poland** — RES Act amendment adding auction support above 1 MW and legalising direct pipelines to bypass grid congestion
- **Spain** — Enagás GdO registry operational since 2023; genuinely the widest origination spread in Europe
- **Amber Gas Corridor** — Baltic/Poland/Finland transit at roughly €2.79–3.46/MWh, useful for logistics cost modelling

### ✅ 7. Roughly 60 named counterparties

Saves real work on your 3-month "10 meetings" KPI. Needs vetting — Maersk is misplaced, Gasum and Titan are conspicuously absent from the maritime list despite being the actual reference players in FuelEU pooling — but as a starting universe it's a genuine head start.

## Part 3 — The framing gap

The report never mentions **3Degrees**. It was written for a generic European merchant trading desk, and Section 7 assumes accordingly:

- That you'll "finalize active accounts across ERGaR, AIB and the UDB" within 30 days. Those are corporate decisions involving membership, legal entity setup and certification scope — **not something a new trader executes unilaterally in month one**.
- That you can offer producers premium prepayment-style BPA terms. That assumes treasury capacity a B Corp climate advisory firm may or may not have.
- That you take title and warehouse positions. Unconfirmed.

It also doesn't know:
- That 3Degrees' existing biomethane business is **voluntary-market corporate**, not compliance — so the real opportunity is the cross-sell path from an existing blue-chip client base that pure-play traders can't replicate
- That the **€1m vs €1.35m** discrepancy exists in your Annex
- That "contracted gross margin **in** CY2027" is ambiguous and may cost you the Q4 2026 contracting window
- That **gross margin is undefined** in your VCA
- That your **probation ends 5 April 2027**, the exact date of the 6-month pipeline KPI

None of that is the report's fault — it wasn't asked. But it means **Section 7 should not be adopted wholesale**, and the parts of your plan that depend on 3Degrees' risk appetite stay open until week one.
